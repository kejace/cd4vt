A converse to the discrete four-vertex theorem

Kristoffer Josefsson, 2026

[ Paper (PDF) | Blueprint | Lean source | Playground ]

Abstract

Tabachnikov1 asked whether every cyclic sequence of positive numbers with at least two local maxima and two local minima is the sequence of radii of the circles through consecutive vertex triples of a convex polygon whose circumcenters lie inside the corresponding vertex angles. We prove this for every n ≥ 4, with extrema counted as plateaus. This is the sharp form: by Dahlberg’s discrete four-vertex theorem2 the condition is also necessary for nonconstant sequences. Locally near every nonconstant admissible sequence the polygon can be chosen to depend continuously (indeed C1) on the sequence, but in general not globally: we give an explicit path of admissible hexagon data along which no continuous choice of realizing polygons exists. The existence and continuity results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib. We also prove the corresponding characterization for circles tangent to three consecutive supporting lines, using a separate tangential-polygon construction and Wegner’s forward theorem.

Formalization

The main results are proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with no sorry and only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The library builds in continuous integration, which also runs #print axioms; a Comparator project checks the statements against an independent copy.

HOW IT WORKS

Closure and local continuation

The solver finds four active edges, solves their closure and turning equations, then opens the remaining edges. During editing it corrects the current lengths while keeping your curvature readings fixed.

Near a singularity or a boundary, the current branch may stop. The previous polygon then fades, and you can explicitly find another realization. A failed numerical search does not mean the profile is unrealizable.

This is a floating-point experiment, with numerical checks rather than formal certificates. The current range is n = 4–24, positive readings from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁶ and curvature ratios up to 10,000.

Constant profiles use a regular polygon. Other admissible profiles have four cyclic readings alternating above and below a common level; those witnesses are ringed on the graph.

    What are the terminal flags? ＋

    After the secondary peak is normalized to κ₀ = 1 and the unique maximum is at H, write Φ(p,q,h,l) = asin(p) + asin(qσ) − asin(σ) − π/2, with σ = √((1−l²)/(h²−l²)).

    The forward flag is Φ₊ = Φ(κ₁, κH−1, κH, κ−1). The backward flag is Φ₋ = Φ(κ−1, κH+1, κH, κ₁). The forward nonnegative case is taken first; the backward case then requires Φ₊ < 0. Equality belongs to the first-transition branches.
